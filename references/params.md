# AP Image Stylization Parameters

## 1. Scene contract

Lock the user's non-negotiable content first:

```text
subject + one visible action + setting + time/weather + narrative reason
```

A still image should freeze one readable moment. Do not describe a sequence of
actions, an edit, or camera movement. If the scene is static, add a subtle
physical event without changing its meaning: fabric caught by wind, a cup just
set down, a door partly opened, or rain reaching a light pool.

## 2. LOOK identity

The opening sentence should join at least four visible style carriers:

- composition or camera distance;
- light source and contrast behavior;
- palette relationship;
- material or medium response;
- atmosphere and emotional tone.

Describe what the image looks like, not where the LOOK came from.

## 3. Framing and depth

Choose one dominant framing system:

- intimate portrait;
- balanced human-scale scene;
- environmental wide;
- monumental scale contrast;
- graphic illustrative composition.

Depth must have a physical reason. Prefer overlap, scale recession, architecture,
terrain, focus falloff, or distance-based contrast change. Add visible fog,
smoke, steam, dust, spray, or snow only when the scene provides that medium.

Keep the main subject readable. Foreground elements may frame or partially
occlude, but may not conceal the story.

## 4. Camera and focus

Use camera language only when it clarifies the image:

- 24–35 mm: environment, speed, monumentality, pronounced perspective;
- 40–58 mm: natural spatial relationships and human-scale scenes;
- 75–105 mm: intimate portraits and compressed layers.

Match aperture behavior to the composition. A wide, information-rich tableau
needs more depth than an isolated portrait. Do not combine deep-focus staging
with extreme shallow-focus wording.

## 5. Lighting and exposure

Name the main source, direction, and quality. Then describe consequences:

- which surfaces receive light;
- where shadows fall;
- what reflects or transmits light;
- how highlights roll off;
- how much information remains in the shadows.

Use fill only when a believable surface, sky, water, fire, window, or practical
source can provide it. Atmospheric glow must affect nearby color, contrast, and
depth rather than floating as decoration.

## 6. Color and materials

Build a relationship, not a list:

```text
dominant family + supporting family + restrained accent + skin/material response
```

Give important surfaces distinct behavior: dry cloth absorbs light, wet stone
reflects, old metal carries oxidation and scratches, translucent fabric passes
light, skin has pores and local color variation, fur and feathers break edges.

## 7. Mood and stylization

Mood must be produced by framing, gesture, light, color, space, and weather. Do
not rely on generic adjectives alone. Stylization may be restrained, strong, or
graphic, but anatomy, contact, scale, and material causality remain coherent.

## 8. Softening budget

Use no more than two strong softening signals among:

- shallow focus;
- diffusion;
- bloom or halation;
- physical fog or smoke;
- visible grain;
- painterly softness.

Reduce the rest to subtle support or omit them. Preserve edges and structure in
the visual center.

## 9. Assembly template

```text
<LOOK identity>. <Subject, action, setting and frozen moment>. <Framing,
aspect ratio, angle and composition>. <Near/mid/far depth and focus behavior>.
<Camera/lens character when useful>. <Motivated lighting, exposure and physical
consequences>. <Palette relationship and material texture>. <Mood and
stylization>. <Medium-specific realism>. <Targeted defect controls>.
```

The final answer is prose, not a visible layer list.
