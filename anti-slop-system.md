# AP Image Anti-Slop System

Use a small set of controls based on the actual scene. Do not paste a universal
negative block.

## Universal core

Aim for:

- coherent anatomy and contact;
- physically readable materials;
- motivated lighting;
- natural tonal depth;
- optical detail instead of digital sharpening;
- intentional composition without duplicated motifs.

## Human scenes

Use when people are visible:

- natural facial asymmetry and age-appropriate features;
- visible skin texture and local color variation;
- correct fingers, joints, grip and weight-bearing posture;
- expressions produced by eyes, brows, mouth and body together;
- clothing that responds to gravity, movement, wind and contact.

Select up to three scene-specific risks: waxy skin, influencer face, malformed
hands, cloned people, costume seams, weightless hair, or unfocused eyes.

## Animals and hybrids

- preserve species-specific skeleton, joints and locomotion;
- keep fur, feather, scale, skin and clothing boundaries coherent;
- make hybrid transitions continuous and functional;
- keep props and environments scaled to the body.

Select up to three risks: mascot proportions, human hands on paws, duplicated
limbs, random animal-part collage, rubber prosthetics, or toy surfaces.

## Architecture and environments

- maintain load, support, access, drainage and circulation logic;
- preserve perspective and repeat patterns without duplication artifacts;
- give weather visible effects on surfaces;
- keep distant structure readable even when contrast falls with distance.

Select up to three risks: impossible stairs, floating structures, copied windows,
decorative pipes, uniform fog, or scale inconsistency.

## Stylized animation

- keep silhouette and pose readable;
- preserve intentional line weight or painted edge language;
- maintain consistent shape simplification across subject and environment;
- use graphic color relationships without flattening depth.

Select up to three risks: glossy 3D finish, mixed rendering styles, noisy detail,
generic character design, or inconsistent perspective.

## Strong atmosphere

Atmosphere must have a source and spatial gradient. It changes distant contrast,
color separation, edge clarity, and light visibility. Avoid a uniform overlay.

## Final wording

State the desired physical result first. Add only meaningful exclusions after
it. For models that respond poorly to negatives, rewrite exclusions as positive
outcomes.
